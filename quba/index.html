Why this exists

Quba started the way most things worth building start: scribbled out on the back of a napkin. A single page. Today's prayer times and the short surahs to recite before you stood to pray. No account. No cost. No noise. Just what you needed.

It grew because answers with their sources attached were hard to find. Every time something proved difficult to look up and trust, that became the next thing to build.

It starts there. It doesn't stop there.

It walks you through each of the five daily prayers: what to say, when to say it, how many rak'ahs, and what every word means in plain language. No prior knowledge needed.

And if you have prayed your whole life, it is still for you. What started as a walkthrough grew into a full reference: AdhkarDu'as99 Names42 HadithThe ProphetsSeerahFastingPurificationZakatMadhhab Differences. It grew because every time an answer proved hard to find with a source attached, that became the next thing to build. Every claim carries its citation. Every ruling names its source.

Quba was built for the Ummah and for everyone finding their way toward it. For the person taking their first prayer. For the person returning after time away, looking for mercy and a way back. For the person asking honest questions and wanting honest answers. It remains free because that is how it was meant to be offered. If it helps you even once, even one prayer, that is why it exists.

About the name

In 622 CE, during the Hijra from Mecca to Medina, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ arrived at a small settlement called Quba. Before continuing into the city, he laid the foundation of the first mosque in Islam. The companions built alongside him, stone by stone.

After settling in Medina, the Prophet ﷺ returned to Masjid Quba every Saturday to pray. He said that whoever comes to it in a state of purity and prays there receives a reward equal to that of Umrah. Masjid Quba still stands in Medina today.

This app carries that name simply out of love for what that place represents. Nothing more.

Where the answers come from

Religious content is easy to get wrong and hard to check. These are the standards Quba holds itself to. They are part of why it took as long to build as it did.

Claims are traceable. Anything presented as Qur'an or hadith carries its reference, so you can verify it yourself rather than take our word for it.

Disagreement is shown, not hidden. Where a narration's grading is disputed, it says so. Where a position is a minority one, it is labelled as one.

No school is smuggled in as "the" answer. Where the madhhabs differ, they are presented side by side and left that way.

Culture is not dressed up as ruling. Common practice and established ruling are two different things, and Quba does not blur them.

It will not give a verdict it has no right to give. The barcode scanner will never tell you a product is halal or haram. It shows you what is in it and what those ingredients are. The ruling is not an app's to issue.

Quba is a reference, not a mufti. For anything that matters, ask someone qualified.
